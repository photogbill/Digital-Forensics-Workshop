# Recover real signal first, quarantine the generators

> **Research snapshot, 2026-09-30** — the tool survey behind FORENSICS_PLAN §12. Decisions taken after it was written: Bill confirmed his RTX 3080 Ti has **16 GB** of VRAM (so the 12 GB caveat below does not apply), and **SAM Audio is accepted as an owner exception** to the licence rule, run through audio.cpp from the `audio-cpp/SAM-Audio-GGUF` weights (§12.9). Licences and figures are as found on that date.

As of September 2026, ATK's Digital Forensics Workshop can cover all four recovery jobs with tools that pass William's licence rule, with one important exception: the most valuable radio capability, listening through a coherent beam and demodulating below the usual FM threshold, exists in no permissively licensed package and has to be built natively in ATK's own DSP library. Across every area, the deterministic or mask-based route recovers real signal and fails by staying unintelligible, while the strong new generative tools (Meta's SAM Audio, UniPASE and GAP-URGENet for speech; SeedVR2, FlashVSR and VOSR 2.0 for video) fail by producing clean, confident content that was never captured. That failure is now measured rather than hypothetical: in the URGENT 2025 challenge a generative enhancer sometimes made low-SNR Japanese sound like English while DNSMOS still scored it well, and Whisper large-v3 emits text on **61.9% of silent clips**. The design that follows has two lanes — a faithful lane of DSP, multi-frame and discriminative models that feeds transcripts, plate reads and decisions, and a watermarked generative lane that only proposes leads — plus routine cross-checking of every recovered word or character against independent tools. Licences bite in predictable places: nearly all mature SDR code (WDSP's new neural NR, SDRangel, ka9q-radio, dsd-neo, KrakenSDR's DOA stack) is GPL and stays at arm's length or serves as a reference, while image and audio-visual models usually fail on their weights and training data rather than their code (Dolphin, AV-HuBERT, CodeFormer, SUPIR and ProPainter are all excluded). For phone video, getting the sender's original file matters more than any model, because only the original can carry an iPhone's four-channel ambisonic track, and no open decoder for that track exists. One hardware assumption needs checking before anything is sized: NVIDIA lists the desktop RTX 3080 Ti at **12 GB**, and the 16 GB part is the laptop GPU.

## Faint radio voice is won before the demodulator, and ATK must build that stage

The published numbers set modest expectations. A phase-locked-loop FM detector buys **2–3 dB** of threshold extension and practical FM-feedback demodulators **5–7 dB**, because both act as narrow tracking filters ahead of the discriminator ([All About Circuits](https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/technical-articles/fm-threshold-extended-demodulators-insights-on-fmfb-and-pll-based-designs)). Model-based demodulation goes further on paper: in Einicke's comparison a first-order extended Kalman filter improves on the discriminator's mean-squared error by **about 12 dB at 7.5 dB SNR**, and a smoother improves again, because the EKF "exploits the signal model whereas the FM discriminator does not" ([Einicke via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/FM-demodulation-performance-comparison-i-FM-discriminator-crosses-ii-first-order_fig5_221925920)). Those are simulated MSE results, not measured intelligibility on real narrowband voice, and no mainstream SDR application ships a Kalman FM demodulator. That gap is ATK's opening: on recorded pre-demodulation I/Q, non-causal processing costs nothing, so an EKF phase/frequency tracker followed by a fixed-lag or Rauch–Tung–Striebel smoother is the strongest native candidate, buildable from the published text without touching GPL code. A circulating claim that an SDR "matched-bandwidth demodulator" reaches "10 to 15 dB" below a radio's threshold comes from a 2026 planning issue, not a measurement ([pdn-soundmodem #508](https://github.com/packet-net/pdn-soundmodem/issues/508)); the zero-risk first step is simply narrowing the channel filter to the occupied bandwidth.

Open SDR code demonstrates cheaper tricks, but almost all of it is GPL. ka9q-radio's optional FM `threshold` mode clamps each sample's phase step to the peak deviation and multiplies it by an IF-amplitude weight p/(p+β·N) with β=0.5, de-weighting the low-amplitude samples that produce clicks ([ka9q-radio fm.c](https://github.com/ka9q/ka9q-radio/blob/master/src/fm.c)), and SDR++ tracks the instantaneous-frequency peak with a sliding FFT ([SDR++ fm_if.h](https://github.com/AlexandreRouma/SDRPlusPlus/blob/master/core/src/dsp/noise_reduction/fm_if.h)). Both are GPL-3.0, so ATK should reimplement them from the description. Synchronous AM with sideband selection, which rides out selective fading, appears in SDRangel (lock range about ±100 Hz) and in WDSP's SAM, which adds a "fade level" option that substitutes a constant carrier during fades ([SDRangel AM demod](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/channelrx/demodam/readme.md); [SDRangel WDSP Rx](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/channelrx/wdsprx/readme.md)); the only PASS implementation is liquid-dsp's MIT-licensed PLL and Costas AM demodulators ([liquid-dsp ampmodem.c](https://github.com/jgaeddert/liquid-dsp/blob/master/src/modem/src/ampmodem.c)). The closed-source SDR# AM and FM co-channel cancellers, which remove a stronger station so a weaker one on the same frequency can be heard, are the commercial benchmark for single-channel interference removal ([SDR# Guide v3.0](https://airspy.com/downloads/SDRSharp_The_Guide_v3.0_ENG.pdf); [rtl-sdr.com](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/sdr-updates-rtl-sdr-enhanced-mode-am-co-channel-canceller-for-mw-dx/)).

After demodulation, neural noise reduction is now standard equipment, and the newest entry is the most radio-specific. **WDSP 2.10 (early September 2026; AetherSDR's integration issue dates the release 9 September) added NNR**, a dual-path RNN that emits complex 5-tap filters on a 512-point STFT at 16 kHz, trained on 85 real off-air HF noise recordings with speech "pushed through a real SSB transmit chain"; the developer reports **+4.3 dB SDR** for the Standard model (261,834 parameters) and **+4.9 dB** for Premium, with 51 ms latency ([AetherSDR RFC #5684](https://github.com/aethersdr/AetherSDR/issues/5684); [WDSP README](https://github.com/TAPR/OpenHPSDR-wdsp)). A spot-check on 30 September found the README on WDSP's GitHub front page still describing v2.00 (1 July 2026), so treat the 2.10 details as resting on the downstream issue and the developer's guide until the upstream release notes are confirmed. NNR is GPL-2.0-or-later with its weights embedded in GPL C files, so ATK can run it only inside a separate GPL receiver, but its architecture and its training recipe (real off-air noise plus a real transmitter chain) can be reproduced natively for NBFM and AM. Every source repeats the same caution. SDRangel's Denoiser documentation says RNNoise "can do a pretty good job at AM, SSB noise reduction however you will need a reasonable SNR… Do not expect it to dig signals out of the noise," and warns that any noise processing placed before the denoiser degrades it ([SDRangel Denoiser](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/feature/denoiser/readme.md)); a ham comparison found Krisp cutting voices off and every tool working best "for those stations with decent signals" ([TheModernHam](https://themodernham.com/noise-suppression-ai-and-ham-radio-a-perfect-mix/)). The PASS-licensed post-demodulation set is RNNoise (BSD), DeepFilterNet (MIT/Apache-2.0), libspecbleach (LGPL-2.1, the spectral engine behind Thetis NR4) and the speech models in the next section ([libspecbleach](https://github.com/lucianodato/libspecbleach/blob/master/LICENSE); [DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet)). NVIDIA's Maxine noise removal, reachable through SDRangel, is proprietary and licensed only for systems with NVIDIA GPUs ([Maxine SDK licence](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/licenses/Maxine_SDK_License_1Apr2021_updated.pdf)). A 2024 air-traffic-control study shows what domain-matched training buys — **+11.09 dB SI-SDR and +0.143 STOI** on real ATC speech — but released no code ([MDPI Aerospace](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/11/7/581)).

FM needs one radio-specific rule for classical noise reduction. With no carrier the discriminator outputs loud, nearly flat noise; under a carrier the noise is "quieted," which is why SDRangel's delta squelch compares demodulated power in bands around 1 kHz and 6 kHz ([SDRangel NFM](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/channelrx/demodnfm/readme.md)). A spectral-subtraction profile captured from the squelch tail would therefore over-subtract badly; the profile should come from pauses inside the transmission, or from a continuous tracker such as WDSP 1.26's "Trained" NSTAT mode ([WDSP README](https://github.com/TAPR/OpenHPSDR-wdsp)). CTCSS helps here too. The tone sits at about **15% of system deviation** ([Wikipedia: CTCSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_Tone-Coded_Squelch_System)), and ka9q-radio already detects it with a Goertzel filter and estimates FM SNR from envelope variance ([ka9q-radio ctcss.c](https://github.com/ka9q/ka9q-radio/blob/master/src/ctcss.c)); because neither measure depends on the speech, both make cheap per-segment quality weights for choosing or combining receivers, although no published study has yet validated them for that purpose.

The KrakenSDR's five coherent channels are the largest untapped lever, because the open ecosystem uses them only to find direction, not to listen. krakensdr_doa computes Bartlett, Capon (MVDR), MEM, MUSIC, ROOT-MUSIC and TNA spectra on Heimdall-calibrated data but has no beamformed audio path, and both projects are GPL-3.0 ([krakensdr_doa](https://github.com/krakenrf/krakensdr_doa)). The beamformer itself is textbook: MVDR holds unit gain toward the target while minimising total output power, with w = R⁻¹s/(sᴴR⁻¹s), and LCMV and null steering extend it to suppress co-channel interferers ([PySDR ch.19](https://pysdr.org/content/doa.html)). SDRuno's two-tuner maximal-ratio combining claims "up to 3 dB" of SNR gain plus better behaviour under fading ([rtl-sdr.com](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/two-antenna-diversity-for-the-sdrplay-rspduo-now-available-in-sdruno/)), which matches 10·log₁₀N array gain; by the same arithmetic five channels offer roughly 7 dB before any fading diversity, an inference not yet measured on Kraken voice. MRC can produce "acceptable SNR even when none of the individual signals are themselves acceptable" ([Wikipedia: MRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal-ratio_combining)), which is exactly the faint-distress case. The recipe for ATK's DSP library is to take phase-calibrated coherent I/Q from the Kraken DAQ, estimate the covariance while the target transmits, steer MVDR at the Capon or MUSIC bearing with nulls on interferers, and hand the beam output to the EKF demodulator described above. Receivers that are not phase-coherent, such as several KiwiSDRs or WebSDRs, can only be combined after demodulation: coarse-align them with the KiwiSDR's GPS timestamps, fine-align by cross-correlation, then select or SNR-weight each time-frequency tile. kiwiclient, the usual recorder for this, has no licence file ([kiwiclient](https://github.com/jks-prv/kiwiclient)), and no tool or paper was found that fuses repeated distress transmissions.

For DMR, P25 and NXDN, the gains are almost all upstream of the vocoder. A 2026 field write-up describes a hardware P25 radio decoding a marginal call cleanly where the software chain recovered "about 4–5 IMBE frames," and blames the software's "no channel equalizer, no soft-decision FEC" ([GopherTrunk Part 13](https://gophertrunk.org/blog/deep-dives/weak-signal-engineering-13-p25-c4fm-gap/)). P25's IMBE frame protects its 88 vocoder bits with four Golay (23,12) and three Hamming (15,11) codes ([Urgent Comms](https://urgentcomm.com/2006/03/01/error-control-coding-for-p25-radios/)), so per-bit reliability fed into those decoders is where the decibels are. In 2025–2026, **dsd-neo** (GPL-3.0-or-later) added Chase-style soft Golay/Hamming decoding, ranked Reed-Solomon erasures, I/Q capture and replay and per-modulation SNR readouts, and **mbelib-neo 2.x** (GPL-2.0-or-later) added the soft-decision vocoder API it depends on ([dsd-neo](https://github.com/arancormonk/dsd-neo); [mbelib-neo](https://github.com/arancormonk/mbelib-neo)); both stay at arm's length. The original mbelib is ISC-licensed but carries an "educational purposes only" patent notice ([mbelib COPYRIGHT](https://github.com/szechyjs/mbelib/blob/master/COPYRIGHT)). The IMBE patent has expired, while AMBE+2 is reported to remain under DVSI patents, and the sources conflict on which patents still apply ([Wikipedia: MBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Band_Excitation)). In practice, decoding DMR's AMBE+2 voice carries patent exposure whatever the code licence, while a native IMBE decoder written from the TIA-102 specification is the clean route for P25. No evidence was found that denoising decoded vocoder audio improves intelligibility; residual vocoder errors show up as frame repeats and mutes, not additive noise.

## Phone clips: secure the original, separate with masks, transcribe with suspicion

The most consequential step for "the quiet person behind the speaker before the clip cuts off" happens before any model runs. iPhone 16-generation Spatial Audio videos carry a stereo AAC fallback, a **four-channel first-order ambisonic track** in Apple's proprietary APAC codec, and a metadata track ([WWDC25 session 251](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/251/); [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255785732)). Apple's Audio Mix can render that ambisonic capture into an "extracted speech" mono foreground stem and an "ambience only" background stem, but only on iOS 26 or macOS 26. FFmpeg cannot decode APAC — its `apac` decoder is an unrelated codec, and PyAV exposes the APAC stream with no codec context ([FFmpeg doxygen](https://ffmpeg.org/doxygen/trunk/libavcodec_2apac_8c.html); [ComfyUI PR #14746](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/pull/14746)). Evidence on messaging apps is thin: the only forensic study found tested WhatsApp in 2020, saw channel counts preserved in most cases, and found large or HD+ Android files fully re-encoded with heavy audio bitrate loss ([UC Denver NCMF thesis](https://www.ucdenver.edu/docs/librariesprovider27/ncmf-docs/victor_thesis_fall2020.pdf?Status=Temp&sfvrsn=67528fb9_2)). The operational rule is therefore to ask the sender for the original by AirDrop, "send as document" or cable. If that original carries APAC, the only working decode today is an Apple device, a step outside the Windows toolkit; render both stems, because a background talker can land in the ambience stem, and avoid "In-Frame" mode, which favours on-camera sources.

On ordinary stereo or mono audio, the PASS-licensed separators are discriminative mask or regression models whose failure mode is leakage rather than invention. SpeechBrain's SepFormer-WHAMR16k (Apache-2.0) is trained on noisy, reverberant two-talker mixtures and reaches **13.5 dB SI-SNRi** on WHAMR! ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/sepformer-whamr16k)). Alibaba's ClearerVoice-Studio (Apache-2.0, pip-installable with Windows instructions, active through 2025) bundles MossFormer2 separation, FRCRN and MossFormerGAN enhancement, and target-speaker extraction ([ClearerVoice-Studio](https://github.com/modelscope/ClearerVoice-Studio)). TIGER (MIT, ICLR 2025) separates two talkers with **under 1 M parameters**, 94.3% fewer than TF-GridNet ([TIGER](https://github.com/JusperLee/TIGER)), while SepReformer is Apache-2.0 but has released only clean WSJ0-2mix weights ([SepReformer](https://github.com/dmlguq456/SepReformer)). Music-vocal models such as the RoFormer and Demucs families put every voice in one stem, so they only strip music or TV from all speech; many community RoFormer weights are CC-BY-NC ([example card](https://huggingface.co/becruily/mel-band-roformer-deux)), and the original Demucs repository was archived in January 2025 ([Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). No source measured any separator on a background talker 15–25 dB below the foreground. The SLT 2026 REAL-TSE challenge, run on genuine conversational recordings, saw speaker similarity fall from about 0.50–0.55 on seen devices to **0.36–0.39 on unseen devices and channels** ([arXiv 2607.15198](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15198v1)) — expect roughly a third of benchmark performance to vanish on a stranger's phone.

Target-speaker extraction is where the newest and riskiest tool lives. **Meta's SAM Audio (December 2025)** selects a source by text ("man speaking"), by a visual SAM 2 mask on a person in frame, or by a time span such as the half-second before the cut, and it separates by flow matching with a Diffusion Transformer in a codec latent space ([arXiv 2512.18099](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18099v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-audio)). That makes it generative; its authors built a separate "Judge" model because SDR-style metrics fail on real recordings, and they flag span prompts as ambiguous when sounds overlap. Its custom SAM License is royalty-free and allows derivatives, but it is gated behind a Hugging Face access request and forbids end uses "related to military or warfare purposes… espionage" ([SAM License](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-audio/blob/main/LICENSE)). It is neither PASS nor non-commercial, so William should decide whether investigative recovery sits clear of those clauses. The clean audio-visual alternative is ClearerVoice's **AV_MossFormer2_TSE_16K**, with Apache-2.0 code and weights, which extracts each visible speaker's voice using face cues ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/alibabasglab/AV_MossFormer2_TSE_16K)); its training data is described only as "large scale open-sourced datasets." The rest of the audio-visual field fails the rule: Dolphin's authors decline to represent its weights as cleared ([Dolphin](https://github.com/JusperLee/Dolphin)), Plug-and-Steer has no licence ([Plug-and-Steer](https://github.com/kaistmm/Plug-and-Steer)), and SoloSpeech is both generative and CC-BY-NC ([SoloSpeech](https://github.com/WangHelin1997/SoloSpeech)). AudioSep (MIT) offers text-queried separation ([AudioSep](https://github.com/Audio-AGI/AudioSep)). Enrolment-based extraction fits a stranger poorly; the only workaround is to cut a short, clean snippet of the background talker and use it as the cue. Lip-reading is a last resort: Auto-AVSR's visual-only WER is **20.3%** even on frontal TED footage, and its weights carry dataset-derived terms ([Auto-AVSR](https://github.com/mpc001/auto_avsr)); AV-HuBERT is licensed for non-commercial research and explicitly bars surveillance and biometric uses ([AV-HuBERT licence](https://github.com/facebookresearch/av_hubert/blob/main/LICENSE)); and VALLR's 18.7% comes from an LLM reconstructing sentences, with no code released ([arXiv 2503.21408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21408)).

Single-channel enhancement has split into the same two camps, and the generative side now has measured hallucinations. On VoiceBank+DEMAND at 48 kHz, ClearerVoice's MossFormer2_SE_48K reaches **SI-SDR 19.36 dB versus 15.71 dB for DeepFilterNet** ([ClearVoice README](https://github.com/modelscope/ClearerVoice-Studio/blob/main/clearvoice/README.md)); DeepFilterNet still has a dependency-free Windows binary but has not had a PyPI release since August 2023 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/deepfilternet/)). GTCRN (MIT) runs at RTF 0.07 on a desktop CPU with **48.2K parameters** and ships through sherpa-onnx ([GTCRN](https://github.com/Xiaobin-Rong/gtcrn)), and nara_wpe (MIT) handles dereverberation by linear prediction ([nara_wpe](https://github.com/fgnt/nara_wpe)). On the generative side, **GAP-URGENet** (MIT code and weights) ranked first in the objective phase of the ICASSP 2026 URGENT challenge ([GAP-URGENet](https://github.com/Xiaobin-Rong/gap-urgenet)), **UniPASE** (MIT code, Apache-2.0 weights) is explicitly built for "low hallucinations" ([arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14606)), and **Sidon** (MIT/MIT) runs "up to 500× faster than real time" ([arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17052)). All three are fully PASS, all three resynthesise speech through a vocoder, and none states Windows support. The hallucination evidence is direct. In URGENT 2025 a purely generative model "occasionally hallucinated spoken content, particularly under low-SNR conditions," Japanese inputs **sometimes came out resembling English**, and "DNSMOS yields high scores even when hallucinations occur" ([arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23212v1)). UniPASE's own table shows near-identical DNSMOS (3.40–3.42) hiding a roughly fourfold spread in content errors — **2.76% dWER for UniPASE against 12.15% for LLaSE-G1** ([arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14606)); LLaSE-G1 is excluded anyway because its download script pulls the CC-BY-NC X-Codec2 ([download.sh](https://github.com/Kevin-naticl/LLaSE-G1/blob/main/ckpt/download.sh)), as is Meta's CC-BY-NC denoiser ([licence](https://github.com/facebookresearch/denoiser/blob/main/LICENSE)). For 8 kHz telephone or radio audio, ClearVoice's super-resolution needs at least 16 kHz effective bandwidth, while VoiceFixer, AP-BWE (2→16 kHz) and UniPASE accept narrowband input by synthesising the missing band ([VoiceFixer](https://github.com/haoheliu/voicefixer); [AP-BWE](https://github.com/yxlu-0102/AP-BWE)). Models that accept a transcript, such as Honee-W's FlowSE and the unofficial Miipher, must never be fed one, since that turns a guess into convincing audio ([FlowSE](https://github.com/Honee-W/FlowSE); [Miipher](https://github.com/Wataru-Nakata/miipher)).

Enhancement can also hurt transcription. With FRCRN, MossFormerGAN and flow models in front of it, "Whisper remains U-shaped and full enhancement falls back to the noisy baseline," and at −7.5 dB the full mask costs **4.2 percentage points of WER** against the best partial correction ([arXiv 2607.11157](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11157v1)). A medical-ASR study found the original noisy audio beat denoised audio in **all 40 configurations** tested ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17562)), and earlier work traced the damage to processing artifacts rather than residual noise, showing that mixing back a scaled copy of the noisy input ("observation adding") restores ASR ([arXiv 2201.06685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06685)).

The ASR step itself carries the highest invention risk in the pipeline. A September 2026 study measured Whisper large-v3 hallucinating on **61.9% of silence, 97.0% of music and 89.9% of non-speech**, because its decoder acts as a language model when acoustic evidence is weak; the usual guards (VAD, `no_speech_prob`, compression ratio, disabling `condition_on_previous_text`) live outside the weights and disappear on serving stacks that skip Whisper's own transcription loop ([arXiv 2609.32560](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32560)). "Careless Whisper" had already found fabricated phrases in about 1% of transcripts, 38% of them harmful ([arXiv 2402.08021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08021)). A clip that cuts off mid-sentence invites exactly this kind of plausible completion. Better-characterised PASS alternatives exist. NVIDIA's Canary-1b-v2 (CC-BY-4.0) publishes both a noise curve (2.18% WER clean, **19.38% at −5 dB SNR**) and a noise-only hallucination rate of 134.7 characters per minute ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2)); Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 is CC-BY-4.0; and Qwen3-ASR, Granite Speech 4.1 and Voxtral Mini 4B are Apache-2.0, as is Cohere Transcribe, though the latter is described as "eager to transcribe silence" ([MarkTechPost survey](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)). Running two or three of these families on the raw clip, a partially enhanced version and each separated stem, and trusting only the words they agree on, is the practical guard. For "who said what," pyannote's community-1 (CC-BY-4.0, gated download) and NVIDIA's streaming Sortformer v2 (CC-BY-4.0, up to four speakers) both pass ([pyannote](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1); [Sortformer](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2)); diarize the separated stem rather than the mixture.

Commercial tools show what to emulate. CEDAR's Forensic Enhance has a **Background** module that raises sounds *behind* the talker — this exact use case ([CEDAR](https://cedaraudio.com/plugins/forensicenhance)). iZotope RX 12 (April 2026) rebuilt Dialogue Isolate and added Scene Rebalance and a Stems View ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/rx-12)), and Acon's Extract:Dialogue 2 exposes dialogue, noise and reverb stems with solo and mute ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/news/acon-digital-releases-extract-dialogue-2---next-gen-dialogue-cleaning-with-real-time-reverb-reduction-65580)). Resolve's local Voice Isolation "doesn't separate overlapping dialogue" ([LightInside](https://www.lightinside.tv/post/ai-voice-isolation-in-davinci-resolve-20-is-it-finally-worth-using)). Adobe Enhance Speech v2 is cloud-only, and its v1 was seen "missing some of the words" on hard audio ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/adobe-enhance-speech-v2-tested/)), while SpectraLayers 12's Voice Enhance openly "uses generative AI" ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/news/steinberg-unveil-spectralayers-12)). A 2025 eight-product shootout concluded that RX and CEDAR "no longer dominate" ([Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/dialogue-noise-reduction-shootout-2025-the-results)); the features worth copying are stems, audible residue and a strength slider, not any single model.

## Visual detail comes from more real frames, not from bigger generative priors

For denoising, the regressive transformers and CNNs remain the faithful default, because they estimate a conditional mean and so blur detail before they invent it. Restormer (MIT) and NAFNet (MIT) cover real-noise denoising and motion deblurring; SCUNet and SwinIR are Apache-2.0; MambaIRv2 (CVPR 2025) is Apache-2.0; and KAIR/DPIR (MIT) adds plug-and-play restoration for a known blur kernel ([Restormer](https://github.com/swz30/Restormer/blob/main/LICENSE.md); [NAFNet](https://github.com/megvii-research/NAFNet/blob/main/LICENSE); [SCUNet](https://github.com/cszn/SCUNet/blob/main/LICENSE); [MambaIR](https://github.com/csguoh/MambaIR); [DPIR](https://github.com/cszn/DPIR/blob/master/LICENSE)). For video, FastDVDnet (MIT) and BasicVSR++'s denoise and deblur weights (Apache-2.0) exploit neighbouring frames, whereas VRT and RVRT are CC-BY-NC and excluded ([FastDVDnet](https://github.com/m-tassano/fastdvdnet); [BasicVSR++](https://github.com/ckkelvinchan/BasicVSR_PlusPlus); [VRT](https://github.com/JingyunLiang/VRT/blob/main/LICENSE)). Classical BM3D is available only through FFmpeg's LGPL-side `bm3d` filter, because the official Tampere packages are "for non-commercial use only," and FFmpeg's `hqdn3d` needs a GPL build ([PyPI bm3d](https://pypi.org/project/bm3d/); [FFmpeg configure](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/configure)). The Mamba models depend on Linux-oriented CUDA kernels, so budget WSL2 for them.

Shadow and low-light recovery should start with operations that add nothing: a linear RAW decode through rawpy (MIT) over LibRaw (LGPL-2.1/CDDL), then exposure lift, CLAHE and exposure fusion in OpenCV, then a regressive denoiser ([rawpy](https://pypi.org/project/rawpy/); [LibRaw](https://github.com/LibRaw/LibRaw)). Phone photos hide an extra source of real highlight detail: Ultra HDR and ISO 21496-1 JPEG/HEIC files carry a gain map, and Google's Apache-2.0 **libultrahdr** decodes it, so a lit sign or plate clipped in the SDR base image can survive in the HDR rendition ([libultrahdr](https://github.com/google/libultrahdr)). Among learned models, three permissive options lead. Retinexformer (MIT) underpins both the NTIRE 2025 low-light winner and the NTIRE 2026 efficient-track winner ([Retinexformer](https://github.com/caiyuanhao1998/Retinexformer)); HVI-CIDNet (MIT, CVPR 2025) comes from the team that took first place in NTIRE 2025 ([HVI-CIDNet](https://github.com/Fediory/HVI-CIDNet)); and DarkIR (MIT, CVPR 2025) handles low light, noise and long-exposure blur together ([DarkIR](https://github.com/cidautai/DarkIR)). Zero-DCE and LLFlow are non-commercial and SCI has no licence ([Zero-DCE](https://github.com/Li-Chongyi/Zero-DCE)). Deblurring follows the same pattern: Restormer, NAFNet, DarkIR and scikit-image's classical deconvolution are PASS, whereas MPRNet is non-commercial, FFTformer and Stripformer have no licence, and the defocus models IFAN and DRBNet are AGPL. Purdue's turbulence models (DATUM, TMT, MambaTM) carry no licence file ([DATUM](https://github.com/xg416/DATUM)), so heat shimmer on a static scene is best handled with real photons alone — register frames with RAFT or SEA-RAFT (BSD-3), keep the sharpest, median-stack them, and deconvolve mildly.

Multi-frame methods are the most defensible way to gain resolution because they exploit real sub-pixel shifts. The permissive set comprises the burst super-resolution networks BSRT and Burstormer (MIT), an MIT Numba reimplementation of Google's Pixel 3 Handheld Multi-Frame Super-Resolution (whose authors recommend WSL on Windows), and STScI's BSD-style drizzle ([BSRT](https://github.com/Algolzw/BSRT/blob/main/LICENSE); [Burstormer](https://github.com/akshaydudhane16/Burstormer/blob/main/LICENSE); [Handheld MFSR](https://github.com/Jamy-L/Handheld-Multi-Frame-Super-Resolution); [drizzle](https://github.com/spacetelescope/drizzle/blob/main/LICENSE.rst)). Licence-plate evidence quantifies the payoff: on UFPR-SR-Plates, recognition rose from **1.7–2.2%** on low-resolution plates to 29.9% with single-image SR and **42.3% with character-position voting across five frames** (best 44.7%), although the dataset itself is non-commercial ([Nascimento et al. 2025](https://valfride.github.io/nascimento2025toward/index.html)). Commercial forensic suites rest on the same idea: Amped FIVE's 140-plus filters include Frame Integration, Perspective Super Resolution and Turbulence Deblurring, and Cognitech's FrameFusion performs sub-pixel multi-frame reconstruction inside a recorded, replayable script ([Amped FIVE](https://ampedsoftware.com/five); [Cognitech](https://cognitech.com/cognitech-video-investigator-64/)).

Single-image and video super-resolution is where licences and invention risk diverge most sharply. The regressive models HAT, DAT, SwinIR and Thera (all Apache-2.0) are the right tools for reading a sign at ×2–×4 ([HAT](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/HAT/blob/main/LICENSE); [DAT](https://github.com/zhengchen1999/DAT/blob/main/LICENSE); [Thera](https://github.com/prs-eth/thera/blob/main/LICENSE)). The GAN upscalers Real-ESRGAN (BSD-3, with MIT-licensed portable Windows NCNN executables) and BSRGAN (Apache-2.0) look sharper but can form wrong strokes ([Real-ESRGAN](https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN)). Diffusion SR with Apache-2.0 code includes DiffBIR, OSEDiff, SeeSR, InstantIR, AdcSR, CCSR, TSD-SR and DreamClear, but their weights inherit Stable Diffusion 2.1 or SDXL terms that the research did not check ([DiffBIR](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/DiffBIR); [OSEDiff](https://github.com/cswry/OSEDiff)); the newest, **VOSR 2.0** (22 August 2026), is a one-step 1.4B DiT whose weights "may have separate terms" ([VOSR](https://github.com/cswry/VOSR)). SUPIR and HYPIR (SupPixel licences), StableSR, ResShift and InvSR (S-Lab), DiT4SR (Pi-Lab) and LucidFlux (FLUX.1-dev non-commercial) are all excluded ([HYPIR](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/HYPIR/blob/main/LICENSE); [SUPIR](https://github.com/Fanghua-Yu/SUPIR)).

For video, BasicVSR++ (Apache-2.0) is still the safest upscaler because flow-aligned propagation carries real information across frames. The 2025–2026 diffusion wave is genuinely permissive — **SeedVR2** (ByteDance, ICLR 2026) has Apache-2.0 code and weights, **FlashVSR** (CVPR 2026, v1.1 November 2025) has Apache-2.0 code on the Apache-2.0 Wan2.1 base, DOVE (NeurIPS 2025) is Apache-2.0 and DLoRAL is MIT ([SeedVR](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR); [HF SeedVR2-3B](https://huggingface.co/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR2-3B); [FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR)) — but it was not built for a single consumer GPU. SeedVR2's official requirement is an H100-80G for 720p clips; only the Apache-2.0 community node, with GGUF/FP8 quantisation, block swapping and tiling, brings the 3B model into 12–16 GB on Windows ([numz node](https://github.com/numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler)). FlashVSR's sparse-attention kernel is documented only on A100, A800 and H200-class cards, and a community node publishes separate 12 GB and 16 GB settings ("tiny" mode, tiled VAE and DiT, fp16) ([naxci1 node](https://github.com/naxci1/ComfyUI-FlashVSR_Stable)). STAR's CogVideoX variant forbids military use and needs about 39 GB ([STAR](https://github.com/NJU-PCALab/STAR)), VEnhancer has no licence and an A100-80G minimum ([VEnhancer](https://github.com/Vchitect/VEnhancer)), and Upscale-A-Video is S-Lab.

Compression artifacts, deinterlacing and stabilisation have clean options with a few GPL traps. FBCNN (Apache-2.0), JDEC (BSD-3) and Google's Knusperli (Apache-2.0) handle JPEG, and STDF and RFDA (Apache-2.0) handle HEVC-style video artifacts ([FBCNN](https://github.com/jiaxi-jiang/FBCNN/blob/main/LICENSE); [Knusperli](https://github.com/google/knusperli/blob/master/LICENSE); [STDF](https://github.com/RyanXingQL/STDF-PyTorch)). FFmpeg's `bwdif`, `yadif` and `w3fdif` deinterlacers sit on the LGPL side, while `nnedi`, `mcdeint` and QTGMC's mvtools chain are GPL; vid.stab was relicensed to LGPL-2.1+, but FFmpeg's configure still lists it as GPL, so in-process stabilisation should use OpenCV or RAFT ([vid.stab RELICENSE](https://github.com/georgmartius/vid.stab/blob/master/RELICENSE.md)). The convenient GUIs and runtimes — chaiNNer, video2x, upscayl, VideoJaNai, vs-mlrt — are all GPL or AGPL and stay at arm's length; the MIT `spandrel` loader is the in-process way to run most of these checkpoints ([spandrel](https://pypi.org/project/spandrel/); [chaiNNer](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/chaiNNer/blob/main/LICENSE)).

Faces and plates are where invention does the most damage. CodeFormer is S-Lab non-commercial, while GFPGAN and RestoreFormer++ are Apache-2.0 GAN or codebook models trained on FFHQ ([CodeFormer](https://github.com/sczhou/CodeFormer/blob/main/LICENSE); [GFPGAN](https://github.com/TencentARC/GFPGAN/blob/master/LICENSE)). RestorerID (Apache-2.0) needs a reference photo, and its base model's identity similarity under heavy degradation is only **0.401**, rising to 0.548 with the reference ([RestorerID](https://github.com/YingJiacheng/RestorerID); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2411.14125)). The 2020 PULSE depixelizer turned Barack Obama into a white man ([The Gradient](https://thegradient.pub/pulse-lessons/)); no face-restorer output should ever be used to identify an unknown person. For plates and text, the recognition stack is PASS — fast-alpr, fast-plate-ocr and open-image-models (MIT, ONNX), plus PaddleOCR, EasyOCR and PARSeq (Apache-2.0) ([fast-alpr](https://github.com/ankandrew/fast-alpr/blob/master/LICENSE); [PaddleOCR](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/LICENSE); [PARSeq](https://github.com/baudm/parseq/blob/main/LICENSE)) — with two cautions: the open-image-models detectors derive from YOLOv9, whose code is GPL-3.0, so their weight provenance needs review ([YOLOv9](https://github.com/WongKinYiu/yolov9/blob/main/LICENSE.md)), and OpenALPR and Ultralytics are AGPL. TATT (MIT) is the one permissive text-SR model ([TATT](https://github.com/mjq11302010044/TATT/blob/main/LICENSE)); plate-SR networks trained with OCR-in-the-loop losses push glyphs toward valid characters, so a wrong character comes out crisp.

The commercial split mirrors the open one. Amped stated in 2021 that FIVE "does not use Artificial Intelligence" for enhancement ([Amped](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2021/11/20/amped-five-does-not-use-ai-and-implements-forensically-safe-algorithms-to-enhance-video-evidence)), and its May 2026 update still added only workflow features ([Amped 40823](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2026/05/20/amped-five-update-40823)). Topaz's Starlight diffusion model (February 2025) explicitly infers missing detail, and Starlight Precise 2.5 now runs locally ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/topaz-labs-unveils-project-starlight-the-first-diffusion-ai-model-for-video-enhancement-302376630.html); [Topaz](https://www.topazlabs.com/news/the-next-gen-release---april-2026)); Topaz-enhanced video was excluded in *State v. Puloka* because the tool "created false image detail" ([ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/litigation-news/2024/fall/court-excludes-aienhanced-videos-trial-evidence/)).

## Broken phone files usually still hold their last seconds

The core fact for "before the clip cuts off" is mechanical. A non-fragmented MP4, MOV or M4A writes its samples into `mdat` continuously and its index (`moov`) only when recording stops. Apple's documentation says a crashed recording without fragments is "unusable (because there is no sample table)," but that with movie fragments — written **every 10 seconds by default** — it is usable "up to the point where the last fragment was written" ([Apple: movieFragmentInterval](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturemoviefileoutput/moviefragmentinterval)). An interrupted Voice Memo kept its `ftyp` and `mdat` with no `moov`, and rebuilding the ALAC magic cookie and the `stsz`, `stco` and `stts` tables recovered "all 19 minutes & 32 seconds" with zero decode errors ([Rossmann Group](https://rossmanngroup.com/services/iphone-data-recovery/voice-memo-recovery)). mp4fixer's author targets "the last half second of video" after a drone crash and advises imaging the whole card, because a copied file stops at its recorded length while valid frames can continue into the following clusters ([mp4fixer](https://github.com/bookkojot/mp4fixer)). No study quantifies how many seconds phones typically lose to unflushed buffers.

The tool landscape is thin. The anthwlock fork of **untrunc** (GPL-2.0) is the only actively maintained open reference-file rebuilder — it had automated Windows builds on 24 March 2026 and a commit on 20 August 2026, and it supports GoPro, Sony XAVC and files over 2 GB ([untrunc](https://github.com/anthwlock/untrunc); [releases](https://github.com/anthwlock/untrunc/releases)) — which makes it a natural arm's-length helper alongside FFmpeg. remoover (ISC) rebuilds `moov` for H.264-in-AVCC plus ADTS AAC using audio frames as the clock ([remoover](https://github.com/imbcmdth/remoover)), and the new stdlib-only Python rebuilder jpfleischer/recover-mp4 needs no reference file but has no licence, so it serves only as a design reference ([recover-mp4](https://github.com/jpfleischer/recover-mp4)). The commercial rebuilders do the same job: Grau Video Repair Tool (Windows; H.264, HEVC, ProRes and camera-specific formats), Stellar ($49.99), Wondershare Repairit ($39.95) and the Treasured bitstream service, with prices taken from a competitor's article that should be checked with each vendor ([Grau](https://main.grauonline.de/video-repair-tool/); [Stellar comparison](https://www.stellarinfo.com/article/best-video-repair-software.php); [Treasured](https://aeroquartet.com/treasured/h265.en.html)). All of them follow one algorithm that ATK can build natively from PASS parts: walk the boxes with `struct`/`mmap` or pymp4 (Apache-2.0); scan `mdat` for length-prefixed H.264/HEVC NAL units with h26x-extractor and bitstring (MIT); inject SPS/PPS taken from a healthy reference clip; rebuild the sample tables; and validate by decoding through FFmpeg as a subprocess with `-fflags +genpts+discardcorrupt` and `ignidx` ([FFmpeg formats](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html); [pymp4](https://pypi.org/project/pymp4/); [h26x-extractor](https://pypi.org/project/h26x-extractor/)). When no reference clip exists, Altinisik and Sencar's method generates valid H.264 parameter sets in **11.3 decoding trials per video on average** across 55K videos ([arXiv 2104.14522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14522)). For reporting and parsing, GPAC/MP4Box (LGPL-2.1) and MediaInfo (BSD-2) are PASS, while Bento4, hachoir and mutagen are GPL ([GPAC](https://github.com/gpac/gpac/blob/master/COPYING); [MediaInfo](https://github.com/MediaArea/MediaInfo/blob/master/LICENSE); [Bento4](https://github.com/axiomatic-systems/Bento4/blob/master/Documents/LICENSE.txt)).

Carving deleted media is weaker still in open source. Defraser, the Netherlands Forensic Institute's frame-level video carver, is BSD but was abandoned in 2016, and its H.264 plugin was sold separately ([Defraser](https://sourceforge.net/projects/defraser/)); PhotoRec is GPL-2 ([TestDisk](https://github.com/cgsecurity/testdisk/blob/master/COPYING)); bulk_extractor has been **relicensed to GPL-3.0-or-later** despite its public-domain reputation ([bulk_extractor](https://github.com/simsong/bulk_extractor/blob/main/LICENSE.md)); and scalpel is Apache-2.0 but unmaintained. A November 2025 paper shows that a native DVR carver is worth building: using published Hikvision (`HKVI`) and Dahua (`DHAV`/`dhav`) frame structures, a Python-plus-FFmpeg method recovered **91.8%** of video across 27 drives, against 82.4–89.2% for commercial tools, with 2.4% false positives — but released no code ([MDPI Information](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/16/11/983)). A DFRWS 2024 decoder-validity technique finds over 99.4% of JPEG fragmentation points within 4 kB, and the same principle transfers to H.264 slices ([DFRWS 2024](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Problem-solved-A-reliable-deterministic-me_2024_Forensic-Science-Internati.pdf)). Magnet WITNESS, formerly DVR Examiner, is the commercial benchmark for proprietary DVR formats ([H11 DFS](https://h11dfs.com/upgrade-your-video-forensics-solution-to-witness/)).

Phone specifics decide where to look. WhatsApp voice notes on Android are plain Ogg Opus `.opus` files that resync on `OggS` pages and decode with BSD-licensed libopus ([Group-IB](https://sudonull.com/post/30099-WhatsApp-in-the-palm-of-your-hand-where-and-how-can-you-detect-forensic-artifacts-Group-IB-Blog); [libopus](https://github.com/xiph/opus/blob/main/COPYING)), whereas Signal attachments are AES-encrypted `.mms` blobs whose key sits behind the Android Keystore, so carving yields only ciphertext without a full file-system extraction ([Plascencia 2026](https://matthewplascencia.substack.com/p/reading-a-droids-signal)). iLEAPP, ALEAPP and voice-memo-forensics are MIT artifact parsers ([iLEAPP](https://github.com/abrignoni/iLEAPP); [voice-memo-forensics](https://github.com/fdelorenzi/voice-memo-forensics)). Deletion windows are short: iOS Recently Deleted keeps items 30 days, and **Google Photos cut its trash window from 60 to 30 days on 4 September 2026** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104967); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/08/google-photos-trash-bin-now-deletes-media-after-30-days-down-from-60-days/)). Audio-only repair is natively implementable framing work — WAV header rebuild, MP3 and ADTS sync-word chaining, AMR frame tables and Ogg CRC resync — because the classic MP3val is GPL-2 and abandoned since 2014, and faad2 forbids "any non-GPL usage" ([MP3val](https://sourceforge.net/projects/mp3val/); [faad2](https://github.com/knik0/faad2/blob/master/COPYING)). AI adds little here, because the data either exists in `mdat` or it does not. Research on corrupted-bitstream recovery (BSCV, NeurIPS 2023; B2SCVR, ACM MM 2025) ships without a licence ([BSCV](https://github.com/LIUTIGHE/BSCV-Dataset)); the best video inpainters, ProPainter and E2FGVI, are non-commercial; and DiffuEraser and VACE have Apache-2.0 code but fabricate pixels ([ProPainter](https://github.com/sczhou/ProPainter/blob/main/LICENSE); [DiffuEraser](https://github.com/lixiaowen-xw/DiffuEraser)). FFmpeg's built-in error concealment, with affected frames marked, is more truthful than any generated frame.

## Two lanes and a validation harness turn generative risk into labelled leads

The standards now support exactly the scheme William's rule implies. SWGDE's August 2025 audio best practice is the first to accept "deep learning filters (trained neural networks)," provided proprietary or unpublished algorithms are "tested before use"; it suggests an order running from distortion repair through separation and reference cancellation, continuous then varying noise, de-reverberation, target balancing and gain, and it requires reviewing "filter residue" so that target speech is not removed, keeping uncompressed intermediates, and documenting every setting for repeatability ([SWGDE 20-A-001 v2.0](https://swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-08-21-Best-Practices-for-Enhancement-of-Digital-Audio-20-A-001-2.0.pdf)). SWGDE's December 2025 video document does not discuss AI, but it formally separates an "investigative lead" — information that should not be the sole basis for charging or court — from evidential analysis ([SWGDE 18-V-001 v2.0](https://swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-12-10-Best-Practices-for-Digital-Forensic-Video-Analysis-18-V-001-2.0.pdf)), and lifesaving recovery fits that lead category. *Puloka* shows why the discipline still matters, since about **15 of every 16 displayed pixels** in the excluded video were algorithm-generated ([Criminal Legal News](https://www.criminallegalnews.org/news/2025/dec/15/when-ai-invents-the-pixels-challenging-ai-enhanced-video-evidence-in-criminal-cases/)). Cohen et al. (NeurIPS 2024) proved that perfect perceptual quality requires **at least double** the restoration problem's inherent uncertainty ([arXiv 2405.16475](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16475)), and a July 2025 "Hallucination Score" paper found hallucination orthogonal to both fidelity and no-reference quality metrics ([arXiv 2507.14367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14367)). The EU AI Act's Article 50 pattern — machine-readable marking of synthetic output — is a sensible model for a watermark even where the Act does not apply ([Amped on the AI Act](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2024/10/23/how-does-the-ai-act-impact-image-and-video-forensics)).

Translated into ATK, the faithful lane holds deterministic DSP, multi-frame methods and discriminative mask or regression models; the generative lane holds SAM Audio, UniPASE, GAP-URGENet, Sidon, bandwidth extension, diffusion SR and VSR, face restorers and inpainting. Every generative output carries a burned-in label and metadata, and transcripts, plate reads and bearings come only from the faithful lane or from generative proposals confirmed against it. The specific guards follow from the evidence above. On the audio side, the subtracted noise stem stays audible alongside a strength control; ASR runs on the raw audio and on partially enhanced versions (observation adding), never only on the fully cleaned one; a word is accepted only when at least two ASR families agree, with words timed at or beyond the clip's end flagged; and because DNSMOS rewards hallucination, content is checked against the noisy original with SpeechBERTScore (MIT) or transcript agreement rather than NISQA, whose weights are CC-BY-NC-SA ([NISQA](https://github.com/gabrielmittag/NISQA); [SpeechBERTScore](https://github.com/Takaaki-Saeki/DiscreteSpeechMetrics)). On the visual side, invention is mapped by running diffusion models over several seeds and displaying per-pixel variance, by diffing each output against a regressive restoration of the same input, by re-degrading the restored image and diffing it against the input, and by voting per character across frames for plates. Every original is hashed on ingest and every chain is recorded as a replayable JSON recipe with tool versions, weight hashes, seeds, regions of interest and time ranges, the way Amped's 2025 Filter Presets and 2026 Assistant Automation work ([Amped 38827](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2025/10/15/amped-five-update-38827)). One test is missing from the literature entirely — no published evaluation feeds speech-absent noise to any named enhancer to see whether it "speaks" — so ATK's validation harness should run it before any model is trusted, in the spirit of SWGDE's tool-testing minimums ([SWGDE 18-Q-001](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/18-q-001-minimum-requirements-for-testing-tools-used-in-digital-and-multimedia-forensics/)). For integrity side-checks, ENF analysis is available in PASS form through ENF-Extractor (MIT) and the MIT-licensed ENF-WHU dataset and code, while the OpenENF tools are GPL and pyenf_extraction is CC-BY-NC ([ENF-Extractor](https://github.com/BitEU/ENF-Extractor); [ENF-WHU](https://github.com/ghua-ac/ENF-WHU-Dataset)); Great Britain publishes open 1-second grid-frequency data, but no open US 60 Hz archive was found and FNET access requires contacting UTK ([NESO](https://www.neso.energy/data-portal/system-frequency-data); [FNET](https://poweritlab.utk.edu/fnet/)).

The hardware question changes only the edges of the plan. NVIDIA's desktop RTX 3080 Ti specification is **12 GB GDDR6X** ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/graphics-cards/30-series/rtx-3080-3080ti/)), while the RTX 3080 Ti Laptop GPU carries **16 GB GDDR6** ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/geforce-rtx-laptops-3080-ti-3070-ti/)); William's notes record 16 GB on a main PC, so `nvidia-smi` should settle which card it is. Every discriminative audio model, regressive image model and file tool above fits comfortably either way, and most audio models run on CPU. The figure matters for Voxtral Mini 4B (stated to fit "a single 16 GB GPU"), for Omnilingual 7B (15–17 GiB, which does not fit either way, although the 8–10 GiB 3B model does), for SAM Audio Large (no published figure), and for SeedVR2 and FlashVSR, whose community nodes publish separate 12 GB and 16 GB settings and lean on the 64 GB of system RAM for block-swap and CPU offload. Several otherwise attractive repositories assume Linux kernels or say so outright — SEMamba and MambaIRv2 through `mamba-ssm`, Handheld MFSR ("We recommend using WSL"), and the generative speech restorers — and WSL2, which runs offline, is the pragmatic answer.

## Gaps the research could not close

Several numbers that would decide priorities do not exist yet. Nobody has measured an EKF or smoother FM demodulator on real NBFM voice — Einicke's 12 dB is simulated MSE — and the ka9q threshold mode, SDR++ FMIF and SDR# co-channel canceller have no published dB figures. WDSP NNR's +4.3–4.9 dB comes from its developer, and no controlled intelligibility comparison of any radio noise reducer exists. No open tool beamforms KrakenSDR channels for listening, combines multiple KiwiSDR recordings, or fuses repeated transmissions, so the roughly 7 dB five-channel figure is arithmetic rather than measurement; soft- versus hard-decision IMBE/AMBE+2 gains and the AMBE+2 patent expiry dates also remain unverified. On phone audio, no benchmark covers a background talker 15–25 dB below the foreground, no in-the-wild lip-reading figures exist, SAM Audio's VRAM and word-level invention rate are unmeasured, the training data behind AV_MossFormer2_TSE's Apache weights is unnamed, and how Signal, Telegram and iMessage treat multichannel or APAC audio is untested, with no open APAC decoder anywhere. Several weight licences went unverified because Hugging Face was partly blocked during the research — TIGER, FlashVSR, DOVE, VOSR, the SD2.1/SDXL-derived SR models, fast-plate-ocr's hub models, VoiceFixer, AudioSR, and the WavLM and w2v-BERT 2.0 encoders under Sidon, UniPASE and GAP-URGENet. No study quantifies how many seconds phones lose when recording is interrupted, and no permissive deep-learning H.264/HEVC error-concealment tool with released weights was found. Finally, the RTX 3080 Ti's VRAM must be confirmed on the machine itself.

## Conclusion

The research inverts a common assumption: ATK's biggest wins will not come from importing the newest model but from native code at the two ends of the chain where open tools stop short — coherent beamforming and model-based FM demodulation on recorded I/Q before the demodulator, and index rebuilding plus tail carving that reads past where the file system says a recording ended. Both are deterministic, both are licence-clean because ATK writes them, and both recover signal that downstream AI can only guess at; the middle of the chain (separation, enhancement, restoration) is now well served by permissive discriminative models that need only integration and a validation harness.

The generative wave of 2025–2026 turned out to be more legally usable than expected — SeedVR2, FlashVSR, UniPASE and GAP-URGENet all carry permissive licences — which makes the labelling discipline, not the licence rule, the real safeguard. The evidence that makes these tools attractive (clean, natural output that standard metrics score highly) is the same evidence that makes them dangerous, because the metrics cannot see the invention. A toolkit that runs generative models beside their faithful counterparts and displays where the two disagree converts that weakness into a usable signal: disagreement marks exactly where the model is guessing.
